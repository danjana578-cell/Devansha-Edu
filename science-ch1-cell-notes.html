Introduction to the Cell
What is a cell? Why is it called the building block?

Every single living thing on Earth — whether it is a tiny bacterium invisible to the naked eye, a tall mango tree, or a complex human being — is made up of cells. The cell is the most basic, fundamental unit of life. Just like a house is built from bricks, all living organisms are built from cells.

📖 Definition — Cell
A cell is the smallest structural and functional unit of life that is capable of performing all the basic life processes independently, such as respiration, nutrition, excretion, and reproduction.
🌍 Real-Life Example
Think of a LEGO set. You can build a castle, a car, or a spaceship using the same basic LEGO bricks. Similarly, nature builds bacteria, plants, and humans using the same basic building block — the cell.

The word "Cell" comes from the Latin word "cellula" meaning "small room". This name was given by Robert Hooke in 1665 when he observed cork under a microscope and saw tiny room-like structures.

Why is the Cell Called the Building Block of Life?

  • Structural Unit: All living organisms are structurally made up of cells.
  • Functional Unit: All life processes (digestion, respiration, etc.) happen at the cellular level.
  • Hereditary Unit: Cells carry genetic information (DNA) that passes from one generation to the next.
  • Reproductive Unit: New cells arise from pre-existing cells through cell division.

🔢 Size of a Cell

  • Most cells are microscopic (average 1 to 100 µm).
  • Smallest cell: Mycoplasma (PPLO) — about 0.1 µm.
  • Largest cell: Ostrich egg — visible to naked eye (~15 cm).
  • Longest cell: Nerve cell (neuron) in humans — up to 1 metre long.

⚠️ Exception to Cell Theory
Viruses do NOT follow cell theory because they are non-cellular (acellular) packets of nucleic acid in a protein coat and cannot reproduce without a host cell.

Types of Cells
Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic & Unicellular vs Multicellular

🦠 Prokaryotic Cell

  • No true membrane-bound nucleus (called nucleoid)
  • No membrane-bound organelles (no mitochondria, ER, Golgi)
  • Small size (1–10 µm)
  • 70S Ribosomes
  • Single circular DNA
  • Examples: Bacteria, Blue-green algae

🌱 Eukaryotic Cell

  • True membrane-bound nucleus with nuclear envelope
  • Membrane-bound organelles present
  • Larger size (10–100 µm)
  • 80S Ribosomes in cytoplasm
  • Linear DNA organized into chromosomes
  • Examples: Plant cells, Animal cells, Fungi

Cell Membrane, Cell Wall & Nucleus
Key cellular boundaries and control mechanisms

1. Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane)

A living, flexible, selectively permeable phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins (Fluid Mosaic Model by Singer & Nicolson, 1972). Controls entry and exit of substances via diffusion, osmosis, and active transport.

2. Cell Wall (Plants Only)

A non-living, rigid, permeable outer layer made of cellulose. Provides structural strength, prevents bursting in hypotonic solutions (turgor pressure), and connects cells via plasmodesmata.

3. Nucleus (Brain of the Cell)

Contains nuclear envelope with pores, nucleoplasm, nucleolus (rRNA synthesis site), and chromatin/chromosomes containing DNA and genes. Directs all metabolic activities and hereditary transmission.

Cell Organelles in Detail
Specialized cellular organs
⚡
Mitochondria
Powerhouse of the Cell
Double membrane; inner cristae folds; matrix with own circular DNA & 70S ribosomes (semi-autonomous); generates ATP via aerobic respiration.
📦
Endoplasmic Reticulum
Internal Transport Highway
Rough ER (RER with ribosomes) synthesizes proteins. Smooth ER (SER without ribosomes) synthesizes lipids and detoxifies toxins/drugs.
🚚
Golgi Apparatus
Packaging & Dispatch Center
Discovered by Camillo Golgi. Parallel cisternae sacs that modify, sort, package proteins/lipids in vesicles, and form lysosomes.
♻️
Lysosomes
Suicide Bags / Cleaners
Single-membrane vesicles with hydrolytic digestive enzymes. Digest waste, bacteria, and burst during apoptosis (cell self-destruction).
🌿
Chloroplasts
Kitchen of the Cell
Found in plants. Double membrane, thylakoid grana with chlorophyll, fluid stroma with own DNA. Site of photosynthesis (glucose production).
💧
Vacuoles
Storage Tanks
Enclosed by tonoplast. Plants have 1 large central vacuole (50–90% volume) with cell sap (turgidity); animals have small temporary vacuoles.

Selected Exam Practice Questions
Rapid fire practice with instant answers
Multiple Choice Questions
Q1.
Which organelle is known as the "suicidal bag" of the cell?
A) Ribosome
B) Golgi body
C) Lysosome
D) Vacuole
✅ Answer
C) Lysosome — contains hydrolytic enzymes that can destroy damaged cells.
Q2.
Which of the following organelles contains its own DNA and ribosomes?
A) Lysosome
B) Mitochondria
C) Endoplasmic Reticulum
D) Golgi apparatus
✅ Answer
B) Mitochondria (and Chloroplasts) — semi-autonomous organelles.
